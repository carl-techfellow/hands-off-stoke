Hands Off
Explain It to the Time Traveler

A write-it-down, hand-it-off, watch-silently game. Genuinely fun to play — the point only reveals itself in the very last sentence.

Group size: 6–30 Time: 10 minutes Needs: paper, a timer, an AI tool Format: in person or virtual

The Card — what to say to the room

Read this aloud
  1. Pair up, across teams.
  2. A gets 60 seconds to write — not say out loud — instructions telling an AI how to explain something totally normal to someone who has never encountered it. Draw a card from the bank, or make one up in the same spirit.
  3. A hands the written instructions to B and then goes completely silent. No talking, no pointing, no "wait, I meant—"
  4. B reads only what's written, pastes it into an AI tool, and reads the result out loud to the room — up to 90 seconds, can re-prompt, gets nothing from A.
  5. Room judges: did it actually work? Would the time traveler get it?
  6. Swap and go again.

The card bank

Explain how to tie a shoe to someone who has never worn shoes
Explain what a Monday is to someone with no concept of a calendar
Explain an elevator to someone from 300 years ago
Explain a text message to a medieval pen pal
Explain how to make a sandwich to someone who has never eaten food cut into pieces
Explain why people clap after a movie to someone who has never seen one
Tip: the funniest results come from instructions that are almost right — the AI takes the literal words and runs, and a missing detail shows up as something genuinely absurd rather than just wrong.

Run Sheet — step by step

Setup

  1. Clear floor space / make sure pairs can spread out.
  2. Confirm paper and pens are out, and every AI tool is open and ready.
  3. Read three card-bank examples out loud so the room hears the register before anyone writes.
  4. Say the rule once, exactly: "Sixty seconds, written only. Once you hand it off, not one more word — no clarifying, no hinting. If it's not on the page, it doesn't exist."
  5. If anyone asks "wait, is this about the project?" — say: "You'll see in a minute." Nothing more.
  6. Pair people up, across teams — count off, don't let people self-select by team.
  7. In each pair, point to one person: "You're A first."

Round 1 (≈4 min)

  1. Say: "A — pick a card, or make one up in the same spirit. Sixty seconds to write instructions for the AI. Go."
  2. Time 60 seconds, visibly.
  3. Call time: "Pens down. Hand it to B. A — you're silent from here."
  4. Say: "B — paste exactly what's written into your AI. Ninety seconds, you can re-prompt, but nothing from A."
  5. Time 90 seconds.
  6. Call time: "B — read it to the room."
  7. Let the room react out loud.
  8. Ask A directly: "Is that what you meant?" — one short answer.

Round 2 (≈4 min)

  1. Say: "Swap. B writes now."
  2. Repeat Round 1's steps with roles reversed.

Land It (≈2 min)

  1. Ask the room: "Would the time traveler actually get it?" Take two or three answers, let people enjoy it.
  2. Then say this once, clearly, and don't repeat it — see below.
  3. Move straight into the work. No lingering on the connection.
"Notice what just happened. You wrote something you were sure was clear. The AI could only use exactly what was on the page — no context, no reading your mind, no filling gaps with what it already knew about you. That's the next forty minutes, except the stranger is a real auditor and the page is your repo."

That's the only connection-to-the-work sentence in the whole session. Say it once, don't repeat it.

Things That Go Wrong

What happensWhat you do
Someone explains the connection too early, hyping round one Head it off before you start: "No spoilers — just play, we'll talk about why after."
A talks during B's turn Call it instantly: "Hands off — that's the whole game."
A's instruction is too long for 60 seconds, cuts off mid-thought Let it run — it's a real finding, and it surfaces itself when the AI's answer is clearly missing the ending A never wrote.
B tries to guess what A "probably meant" instead of reading literally Redirect once: "Only what's on the page."
Someone pastes something real/personal instead of invented Steer it back: "Keep it invented, keep it silly."

Running It Virtually

Same mechanic, same timing. Three adjustments:

  • Breakout rooms, pre-built pairs — post pairings in chat before opening breakouts.
  • A writes in the chat or a shared doc — already the rule here, nothing changes.
  • B shares their screen while working the AI tool, so A and the room see the real output land live — the reactions are half the fun, don't lose them to an unshared screen.

What "Done" Looks Like

The room had a genuinely fun ten minutes, laughed at a few AI outputs, and only in the last sentence connected it to their own repo. If people are still laughing about round two's answer when the closing line lands, that's exactly right — the fun isn't a wrapper to strip off, it's what makes the turn land.
