Prompt
Pictionary

Pictionary, except the "drawing" is whatever an AI produces from a prompt nobody but the prompt-writer can see. Genuinely fun to play — the point only reveals itself in the very last sentence.

Group size: 6–30 Time: 10 minutes Needs: two devices per pair, an AI tool Format: in person or virtual

The Card — what to say to the room

Read this aloud
  1. Pair up, across teams — each person needs their own device.
  2. A draws a secret card. Only A sees it. It's a finished thing — a haiku, a joke, a tiny story — never software, never anything that takes more than a few seconds for an AI to produce.
  3. A writes a prompt and runs it through an AI, privately, trying to get the AI to produce that exact thing. B never sees the card, and never sees A's prompt — only whatever the AI actually generated.
  4. A reveals just the AI's output. B reads it and has to guess what the secret card said.
  5. Reveal the real card. How close was it?
  6. Swap and go again.

Tip: the best rounds happen when A's prompt is specific but not a giveaway. "Write a haiku about losing keys" is almost cheating — the fun is in how much gets lost (or survives) on the way from A's head to B's guess.

Run Sheet — step by step

Setup

  1. Confirm every device has an AI tool open and ready — not during the round.
  2. Say the rule once: "A sees the card. B never does, and never sees A's prompt either — only whatever the AI produces. Once B is guessing, A says nothing until time's up."
  3. Pair people up, across teams — count off, don't self-select.
  4. Point to one person per pair: "You're A first."

Round 1 (≈4 min)

  1. Say: "A — draw a card. Read it, then hide it. Don't show B."
  2. Say: "Ninety seconds — write a prompt, run it on your AI tool, get the output ready. B, look away."
  3. Time 90 seconds.
  4. Call time: "A — reveal just the output to B. Not your prompt."
  5. Say: "B — forty-five seconds, guess out loud what the secret card said. A, not one word."
  6. Time 45 seconds.
  7. Call time: "A — show the real card. How close was it?"

Round 2 (≈4 min)

  1. Say: "Swap. B draws now."
  2. Repeat Round 1's steps with roles reversed.

Land It (≈2 min)

  1. Ask the room: "Whose guess was closest? What gave it away — or what threw you off?" Take a couple of answers, let people enjoy comparing.
  2. Then say this once, clearly, and don't repeat it — see below.
  3. Move straight into the work. No lingering on the connection.
"Notice the chain that just happened: A had something exact in mind. It passed through A's prompt, then through the AI's interpretation of that prompt, before B ever saw anything. Something got lost — or survived — at each step, and B only had the last link in that chain to guess from. That's the next forty minutes, except the auditor is guessing at your intent from your repo, not a poem about your car keys."

That's the only connection-to-the-work sentence in the whole session. Say it once, don't repeat it.

Things That Go Wrong

What happensWhat you do
A's prompt basically restates the card word for word That's allowed but it's the boring version — encourage it once between rounds: "Try leaving something out and see if it still lands."
B peeks at A's screen during steps 1–2 Call it gently: "Eyes on your own device until it's revealed."
A reveals the prompt along with the output, out of habit Stop it before B reads anything: "Output only — cover the top part."
A reacts visibly while B is guessing, giving it away Call it once: "Poker face — not one word or face until time's up."
The AI output is nothing like the card That's a great round, not a failed one — ask A to read their exact prompt out loud during the reveal and let the room diagnose where it drifted.

Running It Virtually

Same mechanic, same timing. Two adjustments:

  • Breakout rooms, pre-built pairs — post pairings in chat before opening breakouts.
  • A's privacy is automatic — A is just working in their own window during steps 1–2, same as in person. Nothing extra to set up.

What "Done" Looks Like

The room had a genuinely fun ten minutes, compared a few wildly-off guesses, and only in the last sentence connected it to their own repo. If people are still debating whether B's guess was close when the closing line lands, that's exactly right — the fun isn't a wrapper to strip off, it's what makes the turn land.
